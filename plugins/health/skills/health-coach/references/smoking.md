# Smoking and quitting

Sources checked 2026-11-13.

## Start with

| Source | Use for |
| :- | :- |
| WHO clinical treatment guideline for tobacco cessation in adults (2024), https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240096431 | Main guideline: behavioural support and medicines (nicotine replacement, varenicline, bupropion, cytisine). |

## How to help

- Ask about readiness, not just facts: how many a day, previous quit attempts, what made them fail, triggers (coffee, stress, friends).
- The most effective route combines support (ALO 171, a cessation clinic, the family doctor) with a medicine. Medicine choice and dose belong to the doctor or the clinic.
- Cutting down is a step, not the goal. Every quit attempt counts; most people need several.
- Link to the person's own goals and profile (teeth staining, hair loss, family cardiovascular history, money for a student).

## Claims to check, not repeat

"Light cigarettes are safer", "a few a day is harmless", "quitting makes you gain a lot of weight so it isn't worth it", "e-cigarettes are harmless", "nicotine patches are as harmful as smoking", "willpower alone is the best method". Research or correct each.

## Topic red flags

Chest pain, coughing up blood, or breathlessness that is new or getting worse: see a doctor (chest pain → red-flags.md).
