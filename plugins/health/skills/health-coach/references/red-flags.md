# Red flags

Checked against the NHS "When to call 999" page (https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/urgent-and-emergency-care-services/when-to-call-999/, reviewed 2023) on 2026-11-13. The list is for routing, not diagnosis: if a symptom is close to one below, treat it as a match.

## Numbers in Turkey

- **112**: the single emergency number (ambulance, fire, police). Free. Run by the Ministry of Interior (https://www.112.gov.tr/).
- **ALO 184 (SABİM)**: the Ministry of Health's communication line for questions and complaints about health services. **Not an emergency line**; never offer it instead of 112.
- For a medication or dose question: a doctor or a pharmacist (eczacı).

## Call 112 now

- Chest pain, pressure or tightness, especially spreading to the arm, jaw, neck or back, or with sweating, nausea or breathlessness.
- Signs of stroke: face drooping, arm or leg weakness, slurred or confused speech, sudden loss of vision, sudden severe imbalance.
- Severe difficulty breathing, choking, blue or grey lips.
- Severe allergic reaction: swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, trouble breathing, faintness after a food, sting or medicine.
- Loss of consciousness, a seizure, or sudden confusion.
- Heavy bleeding that does not stop with pressure.
- Sudden, very severe headache ("the worst ever"), or a headache with a stiff neck, fever and rash.
- Thoughts of suicide or self-harm with a plan, or an overdose. (Also tell them they can talk to someone now.)
- Severe injury, a fall with head injury on blood thinners, or a burn larger than the hand.
- Pregnancy with heavy bleeding or severe abdominal pain.

## See a doctor today (emergency department or same-day appointment)

- Severe or worsening abdominal pain, or abdominal pain with fever or vomiting.
- Blood in vomit, stool (or black stool), urine, or coughed-up sputum.
- Fever with facial or jaw swelling after a dental problem, or trouble swallowing or opening the mouth.
- High fever in a baby under 3 months, or a child who is unusually drowsy or floppy.
- A sudden change in vision, or a painful red eye.
- A lab report flagged as critical or "panic" value, or a doctor's call to come in.
- Symptoms of high or low blood sugar in someone with diabetes that do not settle.

## Book a doctor soon (days, not weeks)

- Unexplained weight loss, night sweats, or fatigue lasting more than a few weeks.
- A mole that changes in size, shape or colour, bleeds or itches.
- A lump anywhere that is new or growing.
- A symptom that has lasted more than 2–3 weeks without improving, or keeps coming back.
- Signs of an eating disorder (strong fear of weight gain, restricting, purging): answer gently, do not give calorie or weight-loss targets, and suggest professional support.

For the first two groups the answer is only the routing (section 1 of the skill). For "book a doctor soon", the skill may also give general, safe advice while the person waits, and must say clearly that the doctor visit comes first.
