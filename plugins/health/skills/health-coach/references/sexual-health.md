## How to answer

- Be plain and non-judgemental. Use correct terms; don't moralise.
- Testing: the family doctor (aile hekimi), a urology, gynaecology or dermatology-venereology clinic. Testing after a new partner is routine care, not an accusation.
- Medication side effects on sexual function (for example finasteride, some antidepressants): explain, never tell the person to stop a prescribed medicine on their own; route to the prescriber.
- Performance worries and anxiety are common and often improve; persistent problems deserve a doctor's visit, not supplements.

## Claims to check, not repeat

"You can tell if someone has an STI by looking", "condoms are not needed in a long relationship without testing", "herbal supplements fix erectile problems", "STIs always cause symptoms". Research or correct each.

## Topic red flags

Severe testicular pain or sudden swelling (emergency department now), sores with fever, pelvic pain with fever, or sexual assault (112 and emergency care; time matters for prevention medicines).
